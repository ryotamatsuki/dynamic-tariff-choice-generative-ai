# Stage 10 Source Verification

Date: 2026-09-12 (JST)

Academic references used in the Stage-10 manuscript were checked against publisher, working-paper, or official repository metadata before inclusion.

- Sundararajan (2004), *Nonlinear Pricing of Information Goods*, Management Science 50(12): 1660–1673. Used for the static fixed-fee / usage-pricing and transaction-cost benchmark.
- Gans (2012), *Mobile Application Pricing*, Information Economics and Policy 24(1): 52–59. Used for the sunk-access / later-pricing hold-up comparison.
- Penmetsa, Gal-Or, and May (2015), *Dynamic Pricing of New Services in Subscription Markets*, Production and Operations Management 24(6): 896–916. Used for dynamic commitment and strategic adoption.
- Muthers and Wismer (2022), *Why Do Platforms Charge Proportional Fees? Commitment and Seller Participation*, Review of Network Economics 21(2): 83–110. Used as the closest conceptual tariff-form / sunk-investment threat.
- Bergemann, Bonatti, and Smolin (2025), Cowles Foundation Discussion Paper 2425, on LLM token allocation, fine-tuning, and optimal pricing. Used for the current LLM-pricing frontier.
- Bergemann and Wang (2025), Cowles Foundation Discussion Paper 2424, on cloud committed spend under demand uncertainty. Used for the dynamic cloud-contract frontier.
- Bhaskaran, Erat, and Mukherjee (2026), Production and Operations Management OnlineFirst. Used for costly digital-service pricing and AI/cloud-related extensions.
- Bichuch and Yaish (2026), arXiv:2608.00823, *Freemium Is All You Need*. Used for dynamic GenAI quality / costly-inference adjacency.

The manuscript does not claim novelty for generic hold-up, generic commitment, two-part tariffs, costly inference, or the mere existence of a mixed equilibrium. The retained novelty statement is the frozen reciprocal state/regime mechanism documented in `docs/freeze/CONTRIBUTION_REGISTER.md`.
