# Repaired Stage 8 — Contribution Register

Date: 2026-09-12 (JST)

## Frozen contribution

The sole headline contribution is the reciprocal architecture-state feedback:

`expected architecture -> continuation rent -> sunk integration -> installed high-use state -> relative architecture profitability -> architecture choice`.

Anticipated metering lowers the high-use installed state relative to anticipated flat pricing on strict `R+`, while the provider's gross metering gain rises with that state. A single fixed-state tariff-switching threshold therefore becomes two expectation-contingent self-consistency thresholds, `mu_M<mu_F`. For `mu_M<mu<mu_F`, neither pure architecture is self-consistent. If integration is architecture-insensitive, `h_M=h_F` and the threshold split collapses.

## Closest-paper distinction

Closest prior-art threats include Gans on future pricing and sunk platform access, Muthers–Wismer on tariff form/commitment and sunk participation, Min–Ryu on sunk investment before later two-part-tariff extraction, Sundararajan on fixed versus usage pricing with metering costs, Ladas–Kavadias–Loch on business-model/pricing choice, Wang–Hu on committed versus contingent pricing and mixed pricing equilibria, and recent AI/cloud tariff papers.

The paper does not claim those ingredients as new. The surviving distinction is the **feedback from the architecture-induced installed state back into the profitability of that same architecture**, producing the two-threshold self-consistency split.

## Institutional interpretation

Enterprise AI/cloud users can make relationship-specific workflow, data, software, and process integrations before later tariff revisions. Fixed/seat, usage/token, and committed-spend pricing structures coexist in related markets. These facts motivate the timing and instrument set.

They do **not** establish empirically that installed integration causally changes later architecture choice. That arrow is a model implication unless separately tested.
