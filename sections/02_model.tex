\section{Model}\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Users, integration, and future usage}

A monopoly provider faces two classes of potential enterprise task integrations, $j\in\{L,H\}$. Each class has unit potential mass. A task of class $j$ draws a sunk integration cost $k$ independently from a uniform distribution on $[0,K_j]$, with $K_j>0$. If it integrates, it receives a current benefit $b_j>0$ before any future repricing. This benefit is non-expropriable by the later tariff decision.

Future gross value from usage $q\geq0$ is
\begin{equation}
 v_j(q)=a_jq-\frac{q^2}{2},
 \label{eq:utility}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
 0<c<a_L<a_H,
 \qquad d\equiv a_H-a_L>0.
 \label{eq:primitive-order}
\end{equation}
The provider incurs real inference cost $cq$. The provider posts an anonymous two-part tariff
\begin{equation}
 T(q)=F+pq.
 \label{eq:tariff}
\end{equation}
The \emph{flat architecture} sets $p=0$. The \emph{metered/hybrid architecture} sets $p>0$ and incurs a real activation cost $\mu\geq0$. The baseline excludes discriminatory menus, arbitrary nonlinear tariffs, competition, congestion, and endogenous model investment.

Conditional on participation, type $j$ chooses
\begin{equation}
 q_j(p)=\max\{a_j-p,0\},
 \label{eq:demand}
\end{equation}
with gross surplus before the fixed fee
\begin{equation}
 S_j(p)=\frac{\max\{a_j-p,0\}^2}{2}.
 \label{eq:indirect-surplus}
\end{equation}
Future participation obeys weak individual rationality: a task participates if and only if continuation utility is weakly nonnegative, and a task with exactly zero continuation surplus participates. This convention matters because the provider will bind the low-use participation constraint on the both-served branch.

\subsection{Timing and equilibrium concept}

The timing is:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item potential tasks simultaneously decide whether to incur their sunk integration costs;
 \item integrated tasks receive the current benefits $b_j$;
 \item the provider observes installed masses $(l,h)$;
 \item the provider chooses the future tariff architecture and tariff parameters permitted by that architecture;
 \item integrated tasks choose future participation and usage.
\end{enumerate}

Users are forward-looking and atomless. The exact integration-cutoff type is indifferent and has measure zero under the uniform distribution. Equilibrium combines rational expectations at the integration stage with globally optimal continuation play by the provider at every installed state. If the provider mixes between architectures, users take the provider's aggregate mixing probability as given when deciding whether to integrate; they evaluate realization-contingent continuation utilities rather than choosing future usage at an average marginal price.

\subsection{Global provider continuation}

Since $S_H(p)\geq S_L(p)$ for all $p$, an anonymous tariff with positive installed masses cannot serve $L$ while excluding $H$. The economically distinct participation regimes are therefore both classes served, only $H$ served, or no service. If $h=0$, the label ``$L$ only'' merely describes the only installed class rather than an additional screening regime; strict $\mathcal R^+$ equilibrium candidates have $h>0$.

Under flat pricing, the provider's globally optimized continuation value is
\begin{equation}
 V_F(l,h)=\max\{\Pi_F^B(l,h),\Pi_F^H(h),0\},
 \label{eq:VF-global}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align}
 \Pi_F^B(l,h)&=(l+h)\frac{a_L^2}{2}-c(la_L+ha_H),
 \label{eq:flat-both-profit}\\
 \Pi_F^H(h)&=h\frac{a_H^2}{2}-cha_H.
 \label{eq:flat-H-profit}
\end{align}
Under metering, both-served profit at a candidate marginal price $0<p\leq a_L$ is
\begin{equation}
 \Pi_M^B(p\mid l,h)
 =(l+h)\frac{(a_L-p)^2}{2}
 +(p-c)\{l(a_L-p)+h(a_H-p)\},
 \label{eq:metered-profit}
\end{equation}
whereas the optimized $H$-only metered continuation earns
\begin{equation}
 \Pi_M^H(h)=h\frac{(a_H-c)^2}{2}.
 \label{eq:metered-H-profit}
\end{equation}
Thus
\begin{equation}
 V_M(l,h)=\max\left\{\max_{0<p\leq a_L}\Pi_M^B(p\mid l,h),\Pi_M^H(h),0\right\}.
 \label{eq:VM-global}
\end{equation}
Appendix~\ref{app:proofs} derives these global values from the unrestricted fixed fee, including negative fixed-fee deviations and the price regions $p<a_L$, $p=a_L$, $a_L<p<a_H$, and $p\geq a_H$. In particular, the $H$-only metered maximum is attained at $p=c>0$, so the open metered strategy restriction $p>0$ does not create a missing supremum. The provider compares $V_F(l,h)$ with $V_M(l,h)-\mu$; equality permits mixing. Hence continuation play is defined even off the both-served equilibrium branch.

\subsection{The strict repaired region $\mathcal R^+$}

The headline theorems are not global over all positive parameters. Define
\begin{equation}
 R_F\equiv\frac{a_H^2-a_L^2}{2},
 \qquad
 l\equiv\frac{b_L}{K_L},
 \qquad
 h_0\equiv\frac{b_H}{K_H},
 \qquad
 h_F\equiv\frac{b_H+R_F}{K_H}.
 \label{eq:state-definitions}
\end{equation}
The interval $[h_0,h_F]$ is a consequence of optimal continuation, not an imposed equilibrium search domain. At every globally optimal continuation, type $L$ receives zero continuation rent: when served, the optimal fixed fee binds the lowest active participation constraint, and when not served its continuation value is zero. Hence its integration cutoff is $b_L$ and every equilibrium candidate has $l=b_L/K_L$. Type $H$'s continuation rent is between zero and $R_F$: an $H$-only optimum extracts its continuation surplus, while on a both-served branch its rent is $S_H(p)-S_L(p)\in[0,R_F]$, with the flat architecture attaining $R_F$. A mixture of optimal continuations gives a convex combination of rents in the same interval. Under the non-saturation restriction below, the high-use integration cutoff therefore implies
\begin{equation}
 h\in[h_0,h_F]
 \label{eq:candidate-interval}
\end{equation}
for every rational-expectations candidate, including candidates induced by provider mixing. This argument uses weak participation for exact fixed-fee attainment, positive installed masses on the candidate set, and the strict non-saturation condition $b_H+R_F<K_H$.

The strict region $\mathcal R^+$ imposes:
\begin{align}
 &0<c<a_L<a_H,\qquad K_L,K_H,b_L,b_H>0,\label{eq:Rplus1}\\
 &l\in(0,1),\label{eq:Rplus2}\\
 &dc<b_H+R_F<K_H,\label{eq:Rplus3}\\
 &c+d\frac{h_F}{l+h_F}<a_L,\label{eq:Rplus4}\\
 &\Pi_F^B(l,h_0)>0,\qquad \Pi_F^B(l,h_F)>0,\label{eq:Rplus5}\\
 &\Pi_F^B(l,h_F)>\Pi_F^H(h_F),\label{eq:Rplus6}\\
 &\Pi_M^B\!\left(c+d\frac{h_F}{l+h_F}\mid l,h_F\right)>\Pi_M^H(h_F).\label{eq:Rplus7}
\end{align}
Only after deriving the complete candidate interval in \eqref{eq:candidate-interval} do we invoke the repaired endpoint-dominance conditions. Appendix~\ref{app:proofs} shows that the relevant both-served-minus-$H$-only profit gaps are strictly decreasing in $h$, so strict dominance at $h_F$ propagates throughout $[h_0,h_F]$. These restrictions do not imply that both-served pricing is globally optimal at arbitrary installed states. Section~\ref{sec:robustness} retains an exact $H$-only counterexample outside $\mathcal R^+$ for that reason.
