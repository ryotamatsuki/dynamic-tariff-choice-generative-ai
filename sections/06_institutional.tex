\section{Institutional Interpretation}\label{sec:institutional}

The model should be read as applying to enterprise AI environments in which both fixed-access and usage-linked charging are feasible. The architecture distinction $p=0$ versus $p>0$ is therefore an economic restriction on the marginal price of future usage, not a claim about the contract menu of any particular vendor.

The timing in Section~\ref{sec:model} represents a setting in which users connect AI systems to data, software tools, internal processes, or task-specific workflows before all future usage terms are known. The model summarizes the current value of such integration by $b_j$ and its heterogeneous sunk cost by $k$. This is a stylized interpretation rather than an empirical estimate of integration costs or repricing behavior.

The interpretation of $\mu$ is deliberately narrow. It is a real cost associated with operating a metered architecture. It should not be read interchangeably as customer budget risk, procurement aversion, a transfer payment, or a reduced-form welfare penalty. Those would be different primitives. Likewise, the feedback from installed integration to the provider's later gain from metering is a theoretical mechanism. Establishing its empirical magnitude would require data on customer-specific integration, subsequent usage, and tariff revisions and is outside the present paper.

The same discipline applies to AI model improvement. The frozen baseline treats $a_L$ and $a_H$ as future value parameters rather than endogenizing model quality. A quality-improvement interpretation is valid only conditionally: if an exogenous improvement moves the certified self-consistency thresholds monotonically relative to a fixed activation cost, the model can generate a transition from flat to the mixed region and then to metering. We do not claim that every improvement in model capability must produce that path.
