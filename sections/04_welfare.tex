\section{Welfare}\label{sec:welfare}

Tariff payments are transfers between users and the provider and therefore do not enter total surplus. Real welfare counts current integration benefits, sunk integration costs, future task value net of inference cost, and the real metering activation cost.

\subsection{First-best benchmark}

For an integrated class-$j$ task facing marginal price $p<a_j$, real future operating surplus is
\begin{equation}
 \omega_j(p)
 =v_j(a_j-p)-c(a_j-p)
 =\frac{(a_j-c)^2-(p-c)^2}{2}.
 \label{eq:omega}
\end{equation}
The unrestricted planner chooses integration and usage directly. For a task with sunk cost $k$,
\begin{equation}
 \max_{x\in\{0,1\},\,q\geq0}
 x\left[b_j+a_jq-\frac{q^2}{2}-cq-k\right].
 \label{eq:first-best-program}
\end{equation}
Since $a_j>c$ in the baseline,
\begin{equation}
 q_j^{FB}=a_j-c,
 \label{eq:first-best-q}
\end{equation}
and integration occurs if and only if
\begin{equation}
 k\leq b_j+\frac{(a_j-c)^2}{2}.
 \label{eq:first-best-integrate}
\end{equation}
This is the only benchmark called \emph{first best}. The comparisons below evaluate decentralized tariff outcomes and are deliberately labeled differently.

\subsection{Decentralized welfare and the fixed-installed-base wedge}

For installed masses $(l,h)$ and architecture $A\in\{F,M\}$ on the certified both-served branch, let $p_F=0$ and $p_M=p^*(h)$. Aggregate welfare is
\begin{equation}
 W_A(l,h)
 =b_Ll+b_Hh-\frac{K_Ll^2}{2}-\frac{K_Hh^2}{2}
 +l\omega_L(p_A)+h\omega_H(p_A)
 -\mathbf 1\{A=M\}\mu.
 \label{eq:welfare}
\end{equation}
The quadratic sunk-cost terms follow from the uniform integration-cost distributions.

Hold $(l,h)$ fixed. The provider's private metering threshold on the quadratic both-served branch is
\begin{equation}
 \mu_P(l,h)
 =\frac{l+h}{2}\left(c+d\frac{h}{l+h}\right)^2,
 \label{eq:private-threshold}
\end{equation}
whereas a social evaluator comparing the same two decentralized allocations meters when $\mu$ lies below
\begin{equation}
 \mu_W(l,h)
 =\frac{l+h}{2}
 \left[c^2-d^2\left(\frac{h}{l+h}\right)^2\right].
 \label{eq:social-threshold}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Fixed-installed-base private/social wedge]\label{prop:W1}
Holding installed $(l,h)$ fixed on the quadratic both-served branch, with $d>0$ and $h>0$,
\begin{equation}
 \mu_P(l,h)-\mu_W(l,h)
 =d h\,p^*(h)>0.
 \label{eq:welfare-wedge}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

The proposition says only that, for the same installed allocation, the monopolist selects metering for a larger interval of real activation costs than a social evaluator comparing those same decentralized allocations. It does not imply that metering is globally excessive in the endogenous game.

\subsection{Restricted architecture commitment}

A separate benchmark commits only the future architecture before integration and allows the provider to reoptimize tariff parameters within that architecture after integration. Under flat commitment the high-use mass is $h_F$; under metered commitment it is $h_M$. The relevant comparison is therefore
\begin{equation}
 W_F(l,h_F)
 \quad\text{versus}\quad
 W_M(l,h_M).
 \label{eq:endogenous-welfare-comparison}
\end{equation}
This is a restricted architecture-commitment benchmark, not the first best.

No unconditional ranking is frozen. The verification suite retains strict-$\mathcal R^+$ examples with both signs for the difference in \eqref{eq:endogenous-welfare-comparison}. Endogenous integration can therefore offset or reinforce the fixed-base pricing wedge. Proposition~\ref{prop:W1} is the sharp analytic welfare result; the full architecture-commitment ranking is sign-indeterminate within the certified baseline family.
