\section{Related Literature}\label{sec:literature}

The paper combines nonlinear service pricing, commitment, sunk integration, and dynamic tariff choice. These ingredients are established separately in prior work and are not claimed as new; nor do we claim novelty for the generic sequence in which early agents form expectations, invest or participate, and later prices are chosen. The narrower candidate contribution is the feedback from an anticipated tariff \emph{architecture} to an installed composition and back into the provider's relative profitability of that same architecture.

\citet{sundararajan2004} studies unlimited-usage and usage-based information-good pricing when administering usage pricing can be costly. We therefore make no novelty claim for fixed-versus-usage pricing, two-part tariffs, or metering costs.

Sequential platform commitment is a particularly important prior. \citet{hagiu2006} studies two-sided platforms in which sellers arrive before buyers, platforms can charge fixed and variable fees, and a platform may choose whether to commit to the later buyer price when setting seller-side terms. An accessible full working-paper predecessor confirms that developer participation and expectations are central and that, without commitment, the later user price responds to the installed developer side. This is much closer to the present timing than a static tariff comparison and rules out any claim that ``expectations $\rightarrow$ early participation $\rightarrow$ later pricing'' is new. The comparison nevertheless does not presently establish whole-game absorption of our more specific object: anticipated flat-versus-metered architecture changes the high-use installed composition, that composition changes the ex-post \emph{relative gain from flat versus metered architecture}, and the resulting feedback produces two self-consistency thresholds and an interior provider-mixing region. A source limitation remains: during the Stage-11 repair we could inspect the publisher abstract and a 60-page public predecessor, but not the complete published 2006 version of record. We therefore do not treat the Hagiu comparison as an exhaustive novelty certificate.

Other dynamic hold-up papers remove further standalone novelty claims. \citet{gans2012} places platform/device access before later mobile-application pricing, so sunk access preceding future pricing is not new. \citet{mutherswismer2022} set the platform tariff before sellers realize investment costs and decide whether to join; the later intermediary entry decision creates hold-up, and proportional fees can serve as commitment. Thus tariff form, seller participation, and hold-up are all prior mechanisms, but the tariff is committed before seller investment rather than selected ex post as a function of the installed seller composition. \citet{minryu2025} have downstream firms invest first and a monopolistic supplier subsequently offer two-part tariffs; their comparison concerns discriminatory versus uniform contracting and directly establishes that sunk investment followed by later two-part pricing is not new.

Mixed pricing choices are likewise not a standalone contribution. \citet{wanghu2014} let competing capacitated firms choose between committed ex-ante pricing and contingent ex-post pricing under demand uncertainty and obtain, among other equilibria, a unique mixed-strategy pricing-regime equilibrium. In the present paper, mixing is therefore not claimed as new; it is only the strategic resolution generated by the expectation-contingent threshold split.

Recent AI and cloud work further rules out application-based novelty. The current March-2026 revision of TSE Working Paper 25-1670 by \citet{bergemannbonattismolin2025}, titled \emph{Menu Pricing of Large Language Models}, studies token-budget menus and committed-spend implementation; the repository does not conflate that revision with the February-2025 version titled \emph{The Economics of Large Language Models}. \citet{bergemannwang2025} study dynamic cloud contracts and committed-spend implementation. \citet{bhaskaran2026} analyze costly digital services including AI/cloud applications, while \citet{bichuchyaish2026} study costly current use and future quality in generative AI. The primitives of our theorem are not AI-specific, so GenAI is a motivating application rather than a source of theorem novelty.

Given the unresolved published-full-text limitation for Hagiu (2006), the present literature audit supports a narrow non-absorption finding only for the materials actually inspected; it does not prove exhaustive novelty.

Our surviving theoretical claim is therefore narrow. On strict $\mathcal R^+$, expected metering lowers high-use integration, while a larger high-use installed state raises the provider's metering gain. This reciprocal state feedback splits one fixed-state architecture threshold into two self-consistency thresholds. Corollary~\ref{cor:collapse} removes the feedback and collapses the split. Generic hold-up, sequential participation before repricing, nonlinear pricing, mixed strategies, AI as an application label, and model improvement are not claimed as standalone contributions.
