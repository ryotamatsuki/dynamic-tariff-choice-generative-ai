\section{Proofs and Algebra}\label{app:proofs}

\subsection{Continuation pricing}

On the both-served branch, type $L$ has the tighter participation constraint, so the provider sets $F=S_L(p)$. Substituting $a_H=a_L+d$ into provider profit gives
\[
\Pi_M(p\mid l,h)
=(l+h)\frac{(a_L-p)^2}{2}
+(p-c)\{l(a_L-p)+h(a_L+d-p)\}.
\]
Differentiation yields
\[
\frac{\partial \Pi_M}{\partial p}
=(l+h)(c-p)+dh,
\qquad
\frac{\partial^2 \Pi_M}{\partial p^2}=-(l+h)<0.
\]
Hence the unique interior metered optimum is
\[
p^*(h)=c+d\frac{h}{l+h}.
\]
At $p=0$ the same expression gives the optimized both-served flat continuation because the fixed fee is already chosen at the $L$ participation constraint. Direct simplification of the profit difference gives
\[
\Pi_M(p^*(h)\mid l,h)-\Pi_F(l,h)
=\frac{[c(l+h)+dh]^2}{2(l+h)}
=\Phi(l,h).
\]
Differentiating this last expression gives equation~\eqref{eq:Phi-derivative}.

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:T1}}

Define
\[
g(h)=b_H+R_F-d\left(c+d\frac{h}{l+h}\right)-K_Hh.
\]
The metered rational-expectations state is a zero of $g$. On $\mathcal R$,
\[
g(0)=b_H+R_F-dc>0.
\]
Using $K_Hh_F=b_H+R_F$,
\[
g(h_F)=-d\left(c+d\frac{h_F}{l+h_F}\right)<0.
\]
Moreover,
\[
g'(h)=-K_H-\frac{d^2l}{(l+h)^2}<0.
\]
Thus $g$ is strictly decreasing and has exactly one zero in $(0,h_F)$. This zero is $h_M$, proving $0<h_M<h_F$. \qed

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:T2}}

Equation~\eqref{eq:Phi-derivative} implies that $\Phi(l,h)$ is strictly increasing in $h$ on the relevant interval. Proposition~\ref{prop:T1} gives $h_M<h_F$. Therefore
\[
\mu_M=\Phi(l,h_M)<\Phi(l,h_F)=\mu_F.
\]
\qed

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:T3}}

Under a pure metered expectation the installed high-use state is $h_M$. The provider then strictly prefers metering when $\mu<\Phi(l,h_M)=\mu_M$ and strictly prefers flat pricing when $\mu>\mu_M$. Under a pure flat expectation the installed state is $h_F$, and the provider strictly prefers flat pricing when $\mu>\Phi(l,h_F)=\mu_F$ and metering when $\mu<\mu_F$. Hence neither pure expectation is self-consistent for $\mu_M<\mu<\mu_F$.

Because $\Phi(l,h)$ is continuous and strictly increasing and
\[
\Phi(l,h_M)<\mu<\Phi(l,h_F),
\]
there is a unique $h^*\in(h_M,h_F)$ satisfying $\Phi(l,h^*)=\mu$. At this state the provider is indifferent and may mix. If $\rho$ is the probability of metering, class $H$ expects continuation rent $R_F-\rho d p^*(h^*)$. Its uniform-cost integration condition is
\[
K_Hh^*=b_H+R_F-\rho d p^*(h^*).
\]
Using $K_Hh_F=b_H+R_F$ gives equation~\eqref{eq:rhostar}.

Positivity follows from $h^*<h_F$. To show $\rho^*<1$, note that the function $g$ in the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:T1} is strictly decreasing and $h^*>h_M$, so $g(h^*)<0$. Therefore
\[
K_Hh^*>b_H+R_F-dp^*(h^*),
\]
which is equivalent to
\[
K_H(h_F-h^*)<dp^*(h^*).
\]
Thus $0<\rho^*<1$, and uniqueness follows from the uniqueness of $h^*$ and the integration identity. \qed

\subsection{Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:collapse}}

If architecture does not change the installed high-use state, write that common state as $\bar h$. Both self-consistency thresholds evaluate the same gain function at the same point:
\[
\mu_M=\Phi(l,\bar h)=\mu_F.
\]
Hence the strict interval between them is empty. \qed

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:W1}}

At a fixed installed state, the provider's private threshold is
\[
\mu_P=\frac{l+h}{2}\left(c+d\frac{h}{l+h}\right)^2.
\]
Using equation~\eqref{eq:omega}, the welfare gain from moving from the flat continuation $p=0$ to the provider-optimal metered continuation $p=p^*(h)$, before paying $\mu$, simplifies to
\[
\mu_W
=\frac{l+h}{2}\left[c^2-d^2\left(\frac{h}{l+h}\right)^2\right].
\]
Subtracting gives
\begin{align*}
\mu_P-\mu_W
&=\frac{l+h}{2}\left[
\left(c+d\frac{h}{l+h}\right)^2-c^2+d^2\left(\frac{h}{l+h}\right)^2
\right]\\
&=dh\left(c+d\frac{h}{l+h}\right)
=dh\,p^*(h)>0.
\end{align*}
\qed

\subsection{Exact outside-region scope guard}

For the parameter vector in \eqref{eq:scope-counterexample}, the both-served flat continuation sets $F=a_L^2/2=8$. Its profit is
\[
(0.1+0.6)8-[0.1(4)+0.6(5)]=\frac{11}{5}.
\]
An $H$-only flat continuation sets $F=a_H^2/2=25/2$, excludes type $L$, and earns
\[
0.6\left(\frac{25}{2}-5\right)=\frac{9}{2}.
\]
The $H$-only advantage is therefore
\[
\frac{9}{2}-\frac{11}{5}=\frac{23}{10}>0.
\]
This calculation establishes only that the both-served candidate is not globally optimal at this point; it does not characterize the complete equilibrium correspondence outside $\mathcal R$.
