\section{Proofs and Algebra}\label{app:proofs}

\subsection{Continuation pricing and global active sets}

On the both-served branch, type $L$ has the tighter participation constraint. Under the weak-participation convention the provider can set $F=S_L(p)$ exactly and retain the full installed low-use mass. Substitution into profit gives
\[
\Pi_M^B(p\mid l,h)
=(l+h)\frac{(a_L-p)^2}{2}
+(p-c)\{l(a_L-p)+h(a_H-p)\}.
\]
Differentiation yields
\[
\frac{\partial \Pi_M^B}{\partial p}=dh-(l+h)(p-c),
\qquad
\frac{\partial^2 \Pi_M^B}{\partial p^2}=-(l+h)<0,
\]
so the unique interior both-served optimum is
\[
p^*(h)=c+d\frac{h}{l+h}.
\]
Direct simplification gives
\[
\Pi_M^B(p^*(h)\mid l,h)-\Pi_F^B(l,h)
=\frac{[c(l+h)+dh]^2}{2(l+h)}
=\Phi(l,h).
\]
The provider's full continuation problem additionally compares this both-served candidate with the $H$-only and no-service values in equations \eqref{eq:VF-global} and \eqref{eq:VM-global}. Strict $\mathcal R^+$ makes the both-served candidate strictly optimal throughout the complete rational-expectations candidate interval $[h_0,h_F]$; no such claim is made at arbitrary installed states.

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:T1}}

Define
\[
g(h)=b_H+R_F-d\left(c+d\frac{h}{l+h}\right)-K_Hh.
\]
The metered rational-expectations state is the zero of $g$. Since $K_Hh_0=b_H$,
\[
g(h_0)=R_F-d\left(c+d\frac{h_0}{l+h_0}\right).
\]
The repaired $\mathcal R^+$ conditions and continuation audit imply the strict lower crossing needed for $g(h_0)>0$. Also, using $K_Hh_F=b_H+R_F$,
\[
g(h_F)=-d\left(c+d\frac{h_F}{l+h_F}\right)<0.
\]
Finally,
\[
g'(h)=-K_H-\frac{d^2l}{(l+h)^2}<0.
\]
Thus $g$ has exactly one zero and it lies in $(h_0,h_F)$. Hence $h_0<h_M<h_F$. \qed

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:T2}}

Equation \eqref{eq:Phi-derivative} implies that $\Phi(l,h)$ is strictly increasing in $h$. Proposition~\ref{prop:T1} gives $h_M<h_F$. Therefore
\[
\mu_M=\Phi(l,h_M)<\Phi(l,h_F)=\mu_F.
\]
\qed

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:T3}}

Under a pure metered expectation the installed high-use state is $h_M$. The provider prefers metering when $\mu<\mu_M$ and flat pricing when $\mu>\mu_M$. Under a pure flat expectation the installed state is $h_F$, and the provider prefers flat pricing when $\mu>\mu_F$ and metering when $\mu<\mu_F$. Hence neither pure expectation is self-consistent for $\mu_M<\mu<\mu_F$.

Because $\Phi(l,h)$ is continuous and strictly increasing, there is a unique $h^*\in(h_M,h_F)$ satisfying $\Phi(l,h^*)=\mu$. If $\rho$ is the probability of metering, the high-use integration condition is
\[
K_Hh^*=b_H+R_F-\rho d p^*(h^*),
\]
so
\[
\rho^*=\frac{K_H(h_F-h^*)}{d p^*(h^*)}.
\]
Positivity follows from $h^*<h_F$. Since $g$ is strictly decreasing and $h^*>h_M$, $g(h^*)<0$, which is equivalent to
\[
K_H(h_F-h^*)<d p^*(h^*).
\]
Thus $0<\rho^*<1$. Uniqueness follows from uniqueness of $h^*$ and the integration identity. \qed

\subsection{Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:collapse}}

If architecture does not change the installed high-use state, write that common state as $\bar h$. Then
\[
\mu_M=\Phi(l,\bar h)=\mu_F,
\]
so the strict interval between the thresholds is empty. \qed

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:W1}}

At a fixed installed state,
\[
\mu_P=\frac{l+h}{2}\left(c+d\frac{h}{l+h}\right)^2.
\]
Using equation \eqref{eq:omega}, the welfare gain from moving from flat pricing to the provider-optimal metered continuation, before paying $\mu$, is
\[
\mu_W=\frac{l+h}{2}\left[c^2-d^2\left(\frac{h}{l+h}\right)^2\right].
\]
Subtracting yields
\[
\mu_P-\mu_W
=dh\left(c+d\frac{h}{l+h}\right)
=dh\,p^*(h)>0.
\]
\qed

\subsection{Exact outside-$\mathcal R^+$ scope guard}

For the parameter vector in \eqref{eq:scope-counterexample}, the both-served flat continuation sets $F=a_L^2/2=8$ and earns
\[
(0.1+0.6)8-[0.1(4)+0.6(5)]=\frac{11}{5}.
\]
An $H$-only flat continuation sets $F=a_H^2/2=25/2$ and earns
\[
0.6\left(\frac{25}{2}-5\right)=\frac{9}{2}.
\]
Hence the $H$-only advantage is
\[
\frac{9}{2}-\frac{11}{5}=\frac{23}{10}>0.
\]
This arithmetic establishes only that the both-served candidate need not be globally optimal outside the certified domain; it does not characterize the full equilibrium correspondence there.
