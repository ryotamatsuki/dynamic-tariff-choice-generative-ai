\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

The model studies integration before future tariff choice. On the certified regular branch, expected metering produces a smaller high-use installed state than expected flat pricing, while the provider's gain from metering rises with that state. The static switching condition therefore becomes two self-consistency thresholds, $\mu_M<\mu_F$. In the intermediate interval, neither pure expectation is self-consistent and the regular branch has a unique mixed resolution. If integration does not respond to tariff architecture, the two thresholds coincide.

The welfare result is narrower. For a fixed installed base, the provider chooses metering over a larger activation-cost region than a social evaluator comparing the same decentralized allocations. With endogenous integration, the model does not imply an unconditional ranking of the two architectures.

The main implication is that the installed base can be shaped by expectations about later pricing and can then alter the provider's later pricing incentive. The quantitative importance of this feedback in enterprise generative-AI markets is left for future empirical work.
