\begin{abstract}
We study tariff architecture when enterprise users make sunk AI-specific integration decisions before a provider can revise future pricing. The provider offers an anonymous two-part tariff; a positive marginal usage price requires a real activation cost. In a quadratic two-type benchmark on a strict region with globally certified continuation play, anticipated metering induces a lower high-use installed mass than anticipated flat pricing, $h_0<h_M<h_F$, while the provider's gross gain from metering rises with that installed mass. One fixed-state switching threshold therefore becomes two expectation-contingent self-consistency thresholds, $\mu_M<\mu_F$. For $\mu_M<\mu<\mu_F$, neither pure architecture is self-consistent; the certified branch has a unique aggregate mixed resolution. Architecture-insensitive integration collapses the two thresholds. Holding the installed base fixed, the provider meters over a larger activation-cost region than a social evaluator comparing the same decentralized allocations, but the endogenous architecture-welfare ranking is not sign-definite. The contribution is the reciprocal feedback from expected architecture to sunk integration and back to the provider's later architecture incentive, not generic hold-up or mixed pricing itself.
\end{abstract}
