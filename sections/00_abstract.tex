\begin{abstract}
We study tariff choice when enterprise users make AI-specific integration decisions before a provider can revise future pricing. The provider offers an anonymous two-part tariff, and use of a positive marginal usage price entails a fixed activation cost. In a two-type benchmark, anticipated metering reduces the installed mass of high-use integrations relative to anticipated flat pricing, while the provider's gross gain from metering rises with that installed high-use mass. The interaction separates the static switching threshold into two self-consistency thresholds, $\mu_M<\mu_F$. For $\mu_M<\mu<\mu_F$, neither pure flat nor pure metered expectations are self-consistent; on the certified regular branch there is a unique mixed resolution. If integration is exogenous or architecture-insensitive, the thresholds collapse. Holding the installed base fixed, the provider meters over a larger activation-cost region than a social evaluator comparing the same decentralized allocations, but no unconditional welfare ranking is claimed once integration is endogenous. The analysis is restricted to a regular region in which both task classes are globally served by the relevant continuation tariffs.
\end{abstract}
